## Dead Phrases — Email

- "I hope this email finds you well"
- "I hope you're having a great week"
- "I'm reaching out to..."
- "Please do not hesitate to reach out"
- "Don't hesitate to..."
- "At your earliest convenience"
- "Looking forward to hearing from you"
- "Thank you for your time and consideration"
- "As per my last email"
- "Just wanted to check in"
- "Just wanted to follow up"
- "Just wanted to reach out"
- "Following up on..." (as opener)
- "Touching base"
- "Per our conversation"
- "Kindly" (just say "please")
- "Regards" / "Warm regards" / "Kind regards" / "Best regards"
- "Sincerely yours" / "Yours truly"

Sign-offs to use instead: "Cheers," / "Thanks," / "Best," — match the register.

## AI Giveaway Punctuation

- em-dash (—) — never use. Rewrite with comma, period, or "and"
- semicolons in casual messages — just use two sentences
- ellipsis for dramatic effect — cut it

## AI Giveaway Structures

- "Not only X, but also Y"
- "Whether it's X or Y, ..."
- "While X, Y" (overused as opener)
- "This means..." / "What this means is..."
- "In other words,"
- "That said," / "That being said,"
- "With that in mind,"
- Lists of exactly three adjectives
- Mirrored sentence pairs ("X is great. But Y is even better.")
- Parallel contrast in a single sentence ("Built for X, not Y" — perfectly balanced opposites)
- "From X to Y" spectrum lists ("from startups to enterprises", "from beginners to experts")
- "This is where X comes in" / "Enter X"
- "And that's [adjective]." as a dramatic fragment ("And that's powerful.", "And that's okay.")
- Rhetorical questions as transitions ("So what does this mean?", "But why does this matter?")
- The recap/summary paragraph that restates what was already said
- Exactly 3 or 5 "key takeaways/insights/lessons" (real lists are irregular)

## AI Giveaway Hedging

- "may" / "might" / "could potentially" in clusters — pick one and commit
- "It's worth considering that..."
- "One could argue that..."
- "There's something to be said for..."

## AI Giveaway Tone

- False enthusiasm not present in source text
- "I completely understand..."
- "Great question!" / "That's a great point!"
- Summarizing what someone just said back to them
- Overly smooth transitions between unrelated points

## Clickbait Title Patterns

Never use:
- "X Things You Need to Know About Y"
- "The Ultimate Guide to X"
- "You Won't Believe What Happened When X"
- "Why X Is the Future of Y"
- "X Is Dead. Long Live Y."
- "How X Is Revolutionizing Y"
- "Everything You Need to Know About X"
- "I Tried X for 30 Days. Here's What Happened."

Write titles that honestly describe the content.

## What to Do Instead

- Say what you mean directly
- Ask the actual question
- State the actual deadline
- Use specific, concrete language
- When in doubt, shorter is better
- Read it out loud — if it sounds like a LinkedIn post, rewrite it
