# Stage 2 — Variations (nine formal registers)

Output: a single markdown file titled `<topic-slug>-seed-for-art-variations.md`.

## Purpose

Take the seed from stage 1 and re-form it through nine distinct voices. Each variation is the **same source material in a different formal register**. The point is the *register-shift*: the source does not change, the voice does. Friction between voices is the work.

Each variation stands alone. Together, they form a library of textures stage 3 can quote from.

## The nine registers

Each section header is `## N — <Register name>`. Each is a self-contained piece in a distinct voice.

### 1 — Inventory

What is on site, as of `<date>`. A bullet list of objects, counts, presences and absences. Begin with the most concrete; end with the most abstract. **Last line, fixed shape:** `All else has been removed, pending the arrival of nothing.` (Adapt the closing clause to the topic, but keep the structure: *all else removed, pending [the absence at the heart of the source]*.)

Tone: dry, clinical, slightly heartbreaking by accumulation. No metaphor. The metaphor is in the list.

### 2 — Minutes (never sent)

A bureaucratic form, drained of content. Use the actual format of the institution involved (committee minutes, court order, hospital chart, kanban card, postmortem template, retrospective notes — whichever fits the source). Replace what would be the substance with `[unknown]`, `*omitted from record*`, `*deferred*`, `n/a`.

End with three flat declarative lines that read like closing stamps:
> Notulen niet verzonden.
> Resident niet geïnformeerd.
> Besluit rechtsgeldig vanaf uitvoering.

(Translate the pattern to the topic's domain. Three institutional finalities, each one line.)

### 3 — Monologue

Speak as a non-human object central to the source. The container, the drawing, the file, the door, the machine, the form. First person.

Six to ten short lines. Sensory and matter-of-fact, not poetic. The object describes its function as if explaining its job. End with two lines of address to a human who is no longer there.

### 4 — Liturgy

Call-and-response prayer form. Six to seven call/response pairs, each two lines:

```
Let us pray for the <thing>.
*That it <do the impossible thing the thing cannot do>.*
```

Each pair targets a different actor (the plan, the drawing, the committee, the citizen, the silence, the absent object…). Close with one line, italics, half-blessing half-joke:
> *Amen, namens de direct belanghebbenden.*

(Adapt the closing to a domain-specific institutional formula.)

### 5 — Glossary

A small dictionary of the domain's specialised vocabulary. Pick 6–8 terms from inside the source. For each term, give two definitions stacked:

```
**<term>** — *n.* 1. <the formal definition>. 2. <the sardonic, lived-experience definition>.
```

The second definition is the work. It is sharp, observed, written from the position of the person on the receiving end. Never mean — wry. The user's experience is encoded here; the word is the case.

### 6 — One breath

A single recursive sentence, ending with `…` (no period). It cascades by repeating one verb construction:
> X is the length of time it takes for an A to become a B to become a C to become a D…

Should run 4–8 lines as printed. Each link in the cascade is a moment in the source's history *as a noun*, never as a date. Ends just past the point where the reader notices it has come full circle.

### 7 — Diary of the [object]

The central object/artifact narrates its own history in five to seven dated diary entries. Each entry: *italic date*, then 2–4 sentences in first person. Tone: matter-of-fact and slightly devotional, as if the object has internalised its institutional life.

Structure: birth → first authorisation → first ignoring → re-authorisation → present. End on the object's current loneliness or final reader.

### 8 — Missing persons report

Format as a real police missing-persons template. Fields:
- **Subject:** <object>
- **Last seen:** <place, date>
- **Distinguishing features:** <three short clauses>
- **Circumstances of disappearance:** <one sentence>
- **Status:** missing
- **Inform:** <user's contact>

The format is the joke. The joke is also the elegy.

### 9 — Instructions

A numbered list — 10 to 14 steps — for *becoming the role the user has been forced into* by the source. Each step is one short imperative line. Use the second person.

Begin with the obvious bureaucratic act. Build to acceptance. End with three lines of identity:
> N. Accept that the organization has no nervous system.
> N+1. Accept that you are it.
> N+2. Report what it cannot feel.

(Translate the three closing identities to the topic's terms — but the *triplet of acceptance* is fixed.)

## A note on use — how to close

One paragraph. State that these are not drafts of a single work; they are nine entry points into one experience. Any can be developed, combined, abandoned, or pressed against its neighbour. End with one sentence of *what is fixed* across all nine — the single load-bearing observation the source rests on.

> Only one thing is fixed: *the resident remembers, the organization does not.* Everything else is form.
