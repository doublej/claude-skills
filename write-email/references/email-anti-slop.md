# Email Anti-Slop

Banned phrases and words. Replace with plain language or delete.

## Dead email openers

- "I hope this email finds you well"
- "Hope you're doing well"
- "I hope you're having a great week"
- "Just wanted to reach out"
- "Just wanted to check in"
- "Just wanted to follow up"
- "I'm reaching out to..."
- "I wanted to circle back on..."

If there's nothing real to say, skip the greeting line entirely.

## Dead email closers

- "Please do not hesitate to reach out"
- "At your earliest convenience"
- "Looking forward to hearing from you"
- "Thank you for your time and consideration"
- "As always, please let me know if you have any questions"
- "Don't hesitate to..."

Replace with: "Let me know if you have questions." or just an actual deadline.

## Dead body filler

- "As per my last email"
- "Per our conversation"
- "Following up on..."
- "Touching base"
- "I just wanted to..."
- "Kindly" (just say "please")
- "Going forward" / "Moving forward"
- "It's worth noting that"
- "It's important to note that"

## Banned sign-offs

- "Warm regards"
- "Kind regards"
- "Best regards"
- "Sincerely yours"
- "Yours truly"

Use: "Cheers," / "Thanks," / "Best," / "—" + name. Match the register.

## AI slop words

dive / deep-dive · delve · unlock · unleash · harness · leverage (verb) · robust · comprehensive · holistic · synergy · pivotal · transformative · cutting-edge · groundbreaking · streamline · empower · elevate · foster · seamless · navigate (metaphor) · landscape (metaphor) · ecosystem (metaphor) · paradigm · multifaceted · facilitate · utilize (just "use") · ensure (just "make sure") · crucial · vital · realm · myriad · plethora · cornerstone · underscore

## AI structures

- "Not only X, but also Y"
- "Whether it's X or Y, ..."
- "While X, Y" (as opener)
- "This is where X comes in"
- "That said," / "That being said,"
- "With that in mind,"
- Mirrored sentence pairs ("X is great. But Y is even better.")
- Triple-adjective lists
- "From X to Y" spectrum lists
- The recap paragraph that restates what was already said

## Punctuation tells

- Em-dash (—) — never. The filter strips these. Don't reintroduce.
- Semicolons in casual emails — split into two sentences.
- Dramatic ellipsis (...) — cut.

## Forced enthusiasm

- "Excited to announce"
- "Thrilled to share"
- "Great question!"
- "That's a great point!"
- Any exclamation mark not earning its place

## What to do instead

- Say what you mean directly
- State the actual deadline
- Ask the actual question
- When in doubt, shorter
- If it sounds like a LinkedIn post, rewrite
