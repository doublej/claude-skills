# DGA Salary (Gebruikelijk Loon) — 2026

**Definition:** Director-Shareholder (DGA) salary must be "reasonable" (gebruikelijk loon) relative to company size, profit, and industry norms. Exceptionally low salary combined with high dividends or loans triggers Belastingdienst challenge. **Accountant-only decision; no automated determination.**

## 2026 Reference Number (medium confidence — FLAG)

**2025 norm:** €56,000 annual salary (reference baseline)  
**2026 norm:** NOT EXPLICITLY CONFIRMED in current sources  

**CRITICAL WARNING:** Before applying any DGA salary threshold in 2026, verify the current norm against [Belastingdienst.nl](https://www.belastingdienst.nl) or consult your accountant. The €56k baseline is from 2025; the 2026 norm may have changed. This document treats €56k as a starting point for discussion only.

## Eligibility & Scope

- **Who:** DGA (Director-Shareholder) of BV; applies to individual and spouse-held structures
- **What:** DGA salary (bruto wages) + any supplementary allowances (car, phone)
- **Comparison basis:** Company profit, company size (payroll, turnover), industry norms, DGA role (full-time vs. part-time)
- **No formal rule:** Geen hard thresholds; Belastingdienst uses "substance over form" test

## Detection Logic (Accountant-Led)

1. **Gather company context:** Profit & loss, revenue, employee count, industry classification (SBI code)
2. **Identify DGA salary + benefits:** Gross salary, car allowance, phone allowance, other taxable benefits
3. **Compare against norm:** Is salary ≥€56k (2025 baseline, verify 2026)? Or at least 80% of industry average for similar role?
4. **Cross-check dividend pattern:** If salary is low (<€40k) AND dividends are high (>profit × 50%), Belastingdienst may challenge
5. **Check loan pattern:** If salary is low + loans are high (>€200k), excessief-lenen + salary mismatch triggers audit
6. **Assess role scope:** Full-time CEO with €30k salary in profitable BV = red flag. Part-time consultant with €25k salary = acceptable.

**Data required:** Payroll record, dividend history (3 years), loan register, profit statement, peer company salary benchmarks (optional, but helpful).

## Pitfalls

- **Arbitrary salary floor:** There is no formal minimum. Belastingdienst challenge depends on overall structure, not salary alone.
- **Loan substitute:** Artificially low salary + high personal loans = attempted dividend avoidance → audit consequence is Box 2 deemed dividend on loans.
- **Dividend avoidance flag:** Salary €50k, profit €200k, dividends €80k → 40% profit as salary, 40% as dividend, 20% retained. Belastingdienst typically accepts this. But salary €30k, dividends €150k, loan draw €20k = all three flags triggered.
- **Industry variation:** Tech startup DGA €60k salary with €500k profit is credible. Manufacturing DGA €60k salary with €100k profit may be questioned.
- **Spouse salary interaction:** If spouse is also employee, their salary must also be reasonable for their role.
- **Year-to-year volatility:** Salary €50k in profitable year, €20k in loss year = acceptable if documented (variable compensation tied to performance). But unexplained year-to-year jumps = audit risk.

## Optimization Tactic

**Annual salary review (accountant responsibility):**
1. Calculate industry salary benchmark (SBI code + company size) or use €56k baseline
2. Set DGA salary at ≥80% of benchmark or ≥€56k (whichever is lower)
3. Structure remainder as dividend (Box 2 tax) or capital retention
4. Document salary decision in meeting minutes (DGA resolution)
5. If salary is below benchmark, require written accountant justification (e.g., "startup phase, reinvestment priority")

**Example salary structure (€100k profit, tech micro-company):**
- Option A: €50k DGA salary + €35k dividend + €15k retained → Belastingdienst likely accepts if documented
- Option B: €30k DGA salary + €55k dividend + €15k retained → Higher audit risk (too-low salary + high dividend)
- Option C: €50k DGA salary + €50k retained (no dividend) → Clean structure, no Box 2 tax, but less cash-efficient
